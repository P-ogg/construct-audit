Construct audit: a simulated literature

Simulated data: 160 invented studies of employee attitudes and performance with planted problems, to show what a report looks like. Planted: self-rated performance partly reflects job satisfaction (jingle); work engagement and job involvement are one variable under two names (jangle); some studies measure identification with a commitment scale (one measure, two constructs), which also makes commitment and identification look like duplicates; all self-reports share a common-source factor (method). Nothing here describes the real constructs.

Comparison constructs: tenure, job satisfaction, age, job performance, supervisor support, job autonomy, organisational commitment, turnover intention.

How to read this report

Profile. For each measure, its pooled correlations with a fixed set of common constructs (the comparison constructs). Two measures of one construct should have similar profiles.

Profile correlation. How alike two profiles are: +1 identical pattern, 0 unrelated, −1 opposite. If interchangeable is the median value when the same studies are randomly re-split between the two measures: what to expect if the measures were the same thing.

Decision rule. A pair of measures differs if its profile correlation is lower than in the random splits, or the average gap between the two profiles is larger, by a permutation test (2000 random splits). The p-values of all tests in the report are corrected for multiple testing (Benjamini–Hochberg); a pair differs when q < 0.05.

Jangle verdicts. Possible duplicate: the two constructs correlate at least 0.85 once measurement error is removed (from at least 5 articles reporting both reliabilities) and their profiles are among the 10% most similar of all pairs. Siblings: correlate at least .40 and similar profiles. Related: correlate at least .40. Distinct: the rest.

One measure, two constructs. The same measure filed under two constructs that correlate .70 or more in at least 2 articles: their relationship may be true by construction.

Minimum evidence. Comparison constructs appear in at least 20 articles; a measure needs 10 articles to get a profile; each profile entry needs 3 articles.

Method

Within the same study, self-report measures correlate on average 0.37 with other self-report measures and 0.08 with measures from other sources (142 articles; other sources: archival (143 articles), other-report (45 articles)). A large gap is consistent with common-source variance.

Findings are evidence for human researchers to judge, not verdicts.
